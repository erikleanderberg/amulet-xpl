FSR Strip Wiring

The force-sensing strip is a resistor that drops from over a megohm to a few kilohms when pressed. One fixed resistor turns that into a voltage the XIAO can read on D0. This page also builds the two power rails everything else on the board hangs off.

Sensor FSR strip · 2 pins, not polarised Reads on D0 / A0 Divider 10 kΩ to GND Rest < 20 counts · hard press 600+

Rewiring from scratch: rails first

  • XIAO GND → − rail (blue stripe), XIAO 3V3 → + rail (red stripe). Two wires. The pulse sensor and the amp both feed off these same rails, so get them in before anything else.
  • 3V3, never 5V. The XIAO's pins are not 5 V tolerant. The 5V pin on the right edge stays empty all day.
  • The FSR has no polarity. Either pin can go to the + rail; the other goes to the junction row. What matters is that the 10 kΩ sits between the junction and ground.

Bands on a 10 kΩ, 4-band resistor: brown · black · orange then gold or silver for tolerance. Brown-black-red is 1 kΩ (too small: rest is fine but a hard press barely moves). Brown-black-yellow is 100 kΩ (rest sits around 85). Anything with a green third band is a megohm and the rest reading lands mid-scale.

Do it

  1. Unplug USB from the XIAO.
  2. Seat the XIAO across the breadboard's centre channel, USB-C at the top, so its two pin rows land in separate row groups.
  3. XIAO GND → − rail, XIAO 3V3 → + rail. Wires ① and ②. These stay for the pulse sensor and the amp.
  4. Push the FSR's two tail pins into two separate breadboard rows, a few rows away from the XIAO. Let the strip lie flat on the desk. Nothing on top of it, nothing bending it.
  5. Red jumper from one FSR pin's row to the + rail. Wire ③.
  6. Pick an empty row to be the junction. Jumper from the other FSR pin's row to it. Wire ④.
  7. Yellow jumper from the junction to D0. Wire ⑤.
  8. 10 kΩ resistor: one leg in the junction row, the other in the − rail. Wire ⑥. Read the bands first: brown, black, orange.
  9. Plug USB back in. Hands off the strip. Open the dashboard and press Re-measure rest.
Why it read ~230 at rest last time, and how to tell which cause it is. With a 10 kΩ pull-down and an untouched FSR (over 1 MΩ), D0 sits at about 0.03 V, which is under 10 counts. A rest reading of 230 counts is 0.8 V, which means the strip was behaving like a 30 kΩ resistor: something was already pressing it. Common culprits: the strip taped down under tension, clamped in a clip, bent at the tail, or lying on a rough edge.

Quick sort: lift the strip fully off the desk and hold it by the tail only. If the rest reading drops under 20, it was preload. If it stays high with the strip in free air, it is the resistor value: pull it and read the bands. If it reads near 500 with the strip disconnected, D0 is floating, which means the junction row isn't the row D0 is in.

What you'll see

1Hands off: under 20 counts, steady. The dashboard's "reads near zero at rest" test passes below 20 and "quiet at rest" wants a standard deviation of 3 counts or less. A wobble at rest is usually a loose junction-row jumper.
2A fingertip: 200 to 400. A thumb, hard: 600 and up. The curve is steep at first and flattens as you press harder. That's the divider, not a fault; the glove threshold logic works on the steep part.
3Let go: back near zero within a second. If it drifts down slowly over several seconds, the strip is still being held by something (tape, an edge, a bend).
4Twenty equal presses, peaks within 10 %. The "20 presses, same peak" test. FSRs are consistent for the same contact area; a moving fingertip is not, so press the same spot the same way.
5100 samples per second. The sample-rate test flags anything off 95 to 105. Off-rate here means the board is stalled, not a wiring issue.
