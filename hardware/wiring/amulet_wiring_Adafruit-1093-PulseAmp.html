Pulse Amp Wiring

Three wires from the Adafruit Pulse Sensor Amped straight to the XIAO. This is the analog heart channel that replaces the DFRobot sensor — it gives a raw pulse waveform, which is what HRV is actually measured from.

Sensor Adafruit 1093 · Pulse Sensor Amped Reads on one analog pin (D1 / A1) Power 3.3 V from the XIAO Signal idles near ~470 on this board

Swapping out the DFRobot sensor

  • Unplug the DFRobot SEN0518. Pull its four wires from D4, D5 and the rails. D4/D5 go free again.
  • Why swap: the DFRobot board returns a computed heart rate once every second or two. The Pulse Sensor Amped streams the raw pulse waveform, so you can find each beat and measure the gaps between beats — that beat-to-beat timing is HRV. The DFRobot path can't give that.
  • Only three wires, and one of them is the analog signal. No I²C, no resistor divider, nothing between the sensor and the pin.

Wiring

The three wires

The lead colours are fixed on this sensor — red, black and purple are moulded onto the cable. Purple is always the signal.

Do it

  1. Unplug USB from the XIAO, and remove the DFRobot sensor's four wires.
  2. Red lead → + rail (3.3 V). Use the 3.3 V rail, not 5 V: the signal rides on the supply, and 5 V would push the analog output past what the pin can take.
  3. Black lead → − rail.
  4. Purple lead → D1.
  5. Cover the back of the sensor — a dab of hot glue, a sticker, or the clear vinyl disc it ships with. The back has exposed contacts that misread if skin, sweat or metal touches them.
  6. Plug USB back in.

Optional, and worth it: a 100 nF cap (you have a bag of them) from D1 to the − rail smooths high-frequency noise off the signal without touching the pulse shape.

What you'll see

1At rest, the signal sits near the middle of the range — about 470 on this board. Not zero, not the top. It idles at half the supply and the pulse swings around that. (Arduino tutorials say 512; this board's ADC reference is 3.6 V, so half of 3.3 V lands near 470.)
2Finger on, held still: a clean repeating wave, one sharp peak per heartbeat. The classic shape — a fast up-stroke, a small notch on the way down. That waveform is the whole reason for this sensor.
3Counted beats match your wrist pulse within ±2 bpm. Count your pulse by hand for 60 s and compare to the peaks the firmware finds.
4Contact pressure has a sweet spot. Too light and the wave is tiny; too hard flattens it. Find where the swing is biggest — that's the pressure the glove strap needs to hold.
5Sample it fast — 250 to 500 Hz. HRV is the millisecond-level gap between beats. The sensor's own reference runs at 500 Hz; the test sketch samples at 500 Hz so beat timing is sharp.

Test it the moment it's wired

Double-click Pulse Amp Test.command (I'll drop it next to the others in ~/Documents/Arduino/fsr_live/). It flashes a 500 Hz reader and draws the live waveform with a running BPM, so you can watch the pulse and run the checks above without opening the Arduino IDE.
