The FSR junction

Three conductors have to meet at one point. Where you physically make that point is up to you — the circuit does not change.

A junction is not a component. It is just a place where wires are all touching each other, so they are all at the same voltage. The 10 kΩ does not have to live on the sensor's leg. It only has to reach that point at one end and ground at the other.

First — the circuit

What the three wires are doing

The FSR is a resistor that falls from over a megohm to a few kilohms when you press it. On its own that tells the XIAO nothing, because the XIAO reads voltage, not resistance. Putting a fixed 10 kΩ underneath it turns the pair into a divider, and the voltage at the point between them moves when you press.

The part that is confusing

The same junction, three ways

All three of these are the drawing above. The FSR lead, the resistor leg and the D0 lead meet; the only thing that changes is what they meet on.

Identical circuits. Nothing about the reading changes between them, which is why the bench numbers still apply to the soldered build.

The build drawing

Three rows, not two

Your harness spec calls for a stripboard scrap of two rows — a 3V3 rail and a GND rail. Cut it one row taller and give the third row to the junction.

  • 3V3 — XIAO 3V3 in, FSR pin A and pulse + out
  • GND — XIAO GND in, pulse −, amp, button, resistor
  • Junction — FSR pin B, D0, resistor leg

One thing to confirm before you solder: the copper strips must run along the rows, in the direction of the dashed arrow. If your scrap's strips run the other way, each row is broken into separate islands and none of this works. Turn the piece ninety degrees.

Order of work

Build it in this order

  1. Cut the scrap three rows tall. Confirm strip direction first, then dry-fit it where it will sit next to the XIAO.
  2. Fit the 10 kΩ first, while the board is empty. One leg into a junction-row hole, the other into the GND row directly above it. Read the bands before you solder: brown, black, orange. Bend the legs over on the back, solder, trim.
  3. Run the GND and 3V3 rails from the XIAO as your harness spec already has them. Nothing about those two rows changes.
  4. Land the FSR pin B lead in a junction-row hole. The sensor end is a 2-pin 0.1 in tail — slip a female Dupont housing onto it rather than soldering the strip, and the sensor stays replaceable.
  5. Run one short lead from the junction row to D0. One wire on the pad, in and out in under two seconds. Route it clear of the D8/D9/D10 amp wires — the junction sits at 10 kΩ to ground and will pick up their switching.
  6. Check before power. Resistance from the junction row to the GND row should read 10 kΩ with the sensor unplugged. If it reads zero, a leg is in the wrong hole or the strips run the wrong way.

Verify

What the dashboard should show

Flash bench3_live and watch the second field of the S line — S,<ms>,<fsr>,<pulse>,<thresh>, 10-bit, so 0–1023.

ReadingMeaning
< 20 at rest, 600+ on a hard pressCorrect. This matches the bench.
