Amulet · glove build · zoned harness

Amulet Glove Harness

Four zones, wired so that as little as possible crosses a joint. The wrist carries the protoboard, battery, button and the 10 kΩ. The palm carries the XIAO, the amp and the LRA. One finger each for the pulse sensor and the force strip. Nine wires cross a bend point in total, and they are ordered along the protoboard so that none of them cross each other on the way.

The three ideas that keep the wire count down

  • Count crossings, not wires. A wire inside a zone is free; a wire across the wrist has to survive tens of thousands of bends. So power is distributed twice: the battery rail lives at the wrist, and the 3.3 V for the two fingers is taken from the XIAO in the palm, not dragged back from the wrist. That alone saves two wires across the wrist.
  • Order the protoboard to match the palm. Left to right on the board: force node, button, V+, ground. That is the same left-to-right order as their destinations in the palm, so the four wrist wires run as a flat ribbon and never cross each other.
  • Your 2-core cable does most of it. The index run is exactly two conductors, so one length of the white 2-core covers it. The middle finger is two plus one. The wrist bundle is two lengths of 2-core.

One thing worth reconsidering

The 10 kΩ at the wrist costs you. It puts the force divider's midpoint at the far end of a 25 cm wire that crosses the wrist. That node sits at 30 kΩ or so, which makes it a decent aerial, and it is the one wire that picks up the amp's switching and mains hum. Moving the 10 kΩ into the palm next to the XIAO fixes both problems: the node shrinks to a few centimetres, the wrist bundle drops from four conductors to three, and you no longer need the mid-wire tap in joint 6. The cost is soldering one resistor inline on a lead rather than flat on a board. If the force channel reads noisy once assembled, this is the first thing to change.
Everything else about this layout is good. The amp and the LRA sharing the palm with the XIAO keeps the I²S wires short, which matters at 1.5 MHz. The battery at the wrist balances the weight away from the fingers. Putting the button at the wrist means it is reachable with the other hand without touching the sensors, and the protoboard gives the battery and switch somewhere solid to anchor to.

Build order

Each stage is testable on the dashboard over USB before the next goes on. The battery is last.

  1. Wrist board. Cut the protoboard, lay in the V+ and GND rails, solder the button and the 10 kΩ, then the battery leads and the switch. Meter the rails: they must read open, not shorted.
  2. Palm cluster. Solder the amp's SD and GAIN loops first while the board is bare. Then XIAO GND to amp GND, the three I²S jumpers, and the LRA to the amp output. Glue the XIAO and amp down to the palm patch so they cannot move relative to one another.
  3. Wrist to palm. Four conductors, kept flat as a ribbon, in the order shown. Anchor both ends within 2 cm of the joint. Plug in USB, press the LRA switch on the dashboard: it should buzz and the force number should not dip while it runs.
  4. Index finger. One 2-core run. Leg A to the red, leg B to the yellow that continues down to the wrist. Solder the D0 stub onto the yellow where it passes the XIAO. Dashboard: a rest reading and a response to bending.
  5. Middle finger. Three conductors to the pulse sensor. Finger on, watch for BEATING.
  6. Battery. Switch off, meter for shorts, then connect. Board boots, dashboard reconnects over Bluetooth once USB is out.

Making it survive being worn

Service loop at every joint

Leave 15 to 20 mm of slack in a gentle S where the harness crosses the wrist and each knuckle. A wire pulled straight across a joint is a wire that will break at the solder joint, usually within a few hundred bends.

Anchor, then joint

Stitch or glue the cable to the fabric within 2 cm of every board. The anchor takes the pull; the solder joint should never feel it. This is the single biggest factor in whether the glove lasts a week or a season.

Route along, not across

Run the finger cables down the side of each finger rather than the middle of the back. The side of a finger changes length far less when you make a fist, so the cable is not stretched every time the hand closes.

Two gauges

The 24 AWG 2-core for the wrist bundle and anything carrying battery current. The AWG30 silicone for the finger signal runs, where flexibility matters more than the milliamps. Silicone survives flexing far better than PVC.
