Amulet Build Spec

Every component, every cable, every solder joint, named and dimensioned. Four zones: a thin wrist module, a palm module, and one sensor on each of two fingers. All cable outside a casing is white 24 AWG two-core; all wiring inside a casing is 22 AWG silicone from the multicolour kit. Written to be handed to another tool as a specification, so nothing is left implicit.

3 · Module stack-up and thinness

The wrist module is the one that has to be thin. The battery and the button sit side by side on the protoboard, not stacked, so the module's thickness is set by whichever of the two is taller, not by their sum.

Wrist module thickness

Floor 0.8 mm casing base
+ 1.6 mm PCB1 protoboard
+ 4.3 mm tallest part on the board: SW1 button, or BT1 at 4.0 mm
+ 1.0 mm clearance
+ 0.8 mm lid, with a hole for the plunger
= 8.5 mm total

To lose 2 mm: use 1.0 mm protoboard and a 3 mm cell such as a 302030, and let the plunger stand proud through the lid rather than clearing it internally.

Wrist module footprint

Protoboard 45 × 22 mm holds, left to right: SW1 button, R1, then BT1 lying on the board with SW2 beyond it. Casing outside dimensions about 52 × 28 × 8.5 mm with 1.5 mm walls and rounded corners.

Long axis runs along the forearm, not across it. Across is what makes a wrist module feel bulky.

Palm module

MCU1 and AMP1 sit side by side, both flat. Height is set by the XIAO at 3.5 mm, or about 5 mm over its antenna end. With a 0.8 mm floor, 1 mm clearance and a 0.8 mm lid that is ≈ 7.5 mm.

Footprint: 21 + 17.5 mm side by side plus a 3 mm gap and 2 mm margins is about 46 × 24 mm. LRA1 sits under or beside them, against the skin.

Cable passages

Each casing needs one passage per cable: 2 in the palm floor (cables A and B), 3 in the palm lid (C, D, E), 2 in the wrist lid (A and B).

Size each at 4.5 mm for a 3.5 mm jacket, or 4.8 mm if 3D printed. Make them open slots with a C shape so an already-soldered harness drops in sideways. Break every edge.

LRA1's dimensions are unknown to me, so the palm figure assumes it is no thicker than the XIAO. A typical coin LRA is 10 mm across and 3.4 mm thick, which would fit. If the Drake is taller, the palm module's height is set by the LRA instead. Measure it.

4 · Cable schedule — everything outside a casing

Five runs, all the same white 24 AWG two-core. Ten conductors, nine used and one spare. Cut every run 15 to 20 mm longer than the straight-line distance so each one carries a service loop at the joint it crosses.

Why the pulse sensor gets two cables instead of one three-conductor run. It needs three conductors and the cable has two, so a second run is unavoidable. Splitting them as power in cable D and signal in cable E rather than three-plus-one is free and it keeps the sensitive analogue signal out of the same jacket as the supply. The spare core in cable E can later become a shield drain, grounded at the palm end only, or a second finger sensor.
Mark the jackets. All five cables look identical. Put one band of tape near each end: one band for A, two for B, three for C, and so on. Do it before the first joint is soldered, not after.

7 · Assembly order

Every stage is testable on the dashboard over USB before the next one goes on. The battery is connected last.

  1. Mark the five cables. Tape bands, one through five, both ends. Cut each to the length in section 4 plus 20 mm.
  2. AMP1 jumpers. Joints P19 and P20, while the amp board is bare and easy to hold.
  3. Palm module dry build. MCU1 and AMP1 glued to the casing floor, then P5, P6, P16, P17, P18, P21, P22. Plug in USB. The LRA should buzz from the dashboard switch, and the board should enumerate.
  4. PCB1. Cut the board, lay the two rails, solder SW1, R1, SW2 and the battery leads: W1 to W7. Meter V+ to GND: open, not shorted.
  5. Cables A and B. W8 to W11 at the wrist end, then P1 to P4, P11, P12, P14 at the palm end. Leave a service loop at the wrist. Plug in USB: force and button nets are now continuous, and the force channel should read a rest value.
  6. Cable C and FS1. P9, P13, then I1 and I2. Bend the strip, watch the reading move.
  7. Cables D and E and PPG1. P7, P10, P15, then M1 to M4. Finger on the sensor, watch for BEATING on the dashboard.
  8. Close the casings, then connect BT1 with SW2 off. Meter for a short first. Switch on, unplug USB, and the bridge should pick the board up over Bluetooth within eight seconds.
Before BT1 is connected, three meter checks. V+ rail to GND rail: open. MCU1 BAT+ to BAT−: open. AMP1 Vin to GND: open. A dead short across a lithium cell is the one mistake in this build that damages parts rather than just not working.

8 · Making it survive being worn

Service loops

15 to 20 mm of slack in a gentle S wherever a cable crosses the wrist or a knuckle. A cable pulled straight across a joint breaks at the solder joint, usually within a few hundred bends.

Anchor before the joint

Stitch or glue each cable to the fabric within 20 mm of every casing passage and every sensor. The anchor takes the pull. The joint should never feel it.

Route along, not across

Run the finger cables down the side of each finger, not the middle of the back. The side changes length far less when the hand closes, so the cable is not stretched on every grip.

Two gauges, on purpose

24 AWG two-core outside, where the jacket takes the abrasion and the pull. 22 AWG silicone inside, where flexibility and easy stripping matter and nothing rubs. PVC stiffens and cracks with flexing; silicone does not.
