Amulet XPL · harness · joints 11–12

Which button legs

A 6 mm tactile switch has four legs but only two circuits. Two of those legs are already joined inside the case, which is what has gone wrong on your board.

The rule: solder to two diagonally opposite legs. Diagonal is the only pairing that is right no matter which way round the switch sits.

Why it failed

Two of the legs are the same wire

Inside the case, the legs are bonded in pairs. Each pair is one conductor before you solder anything. Pressing the button is what bridges one pair to the other — that is the whole mechanism.

What your board has now: both wires landed on one pair, so D2 is wired straight to GND through the internal bar. The firmware reads it LOW forever and no press can ever change it — which is precisely what the scope showed.

Right and wrong

Same side kills it, diagonal works

Why diagonal, specifically

It is right whichever way the switch sits

You do not have to work out which way the internal bars run, and you cannot see them anyway. A diagonal always crosses them, so it always takes one leg from each pair.

The fix

What to actually do

  1. Leave one wire where it is. Say it is on leg A — that one is fine.
  2. Desolder the other wire from the leg beside it and move it to the leg diagonally across the body. Not the one next to it, not the one directly opposite along the same edge — the far corner.
  3. Either wire can be either side. The switch has no polarity: one leg goes to the GND row, the other to D2, and it does not matter which is which.
  4. Watch the scope. The button panel will drop its warning and show released the moment D2 is free, then light up on each press with the count stepping up.
