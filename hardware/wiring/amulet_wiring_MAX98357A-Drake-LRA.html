Amp & LRA Wiring

The XIAO sends digital audio over three I²S wires to the MAX98357A, which turns it into a bridged drive for the Drake linear resonant actuator. Seven wires to the amp, two to the coil, and the amp's two strap pins set once.

Amp Adafruit 3006 · MAX98357A I²S Actuator TITAN Drake HF · 8 Ω · 0.5–7 Vpp · resonance ~160 Hz Pins D8 DIN · D9 BCLK · D10 LRC Power 3.3 V · never 5 V

Three things that are easy to get wrong

  • Amp power is 3.0 to 3.3 V, never 5 V. The amp is bridge-tied and swings about twice its supply across the coil. At 3.3 V that's 6.6 Vpp, under the Drake's 7 Vpp rating. At 5 V it's 10 Vpp, which cooks the coil. For these short, bounded tests the + rail is fine; if you have a separate 3.0 to 3.3 V bench supply, use it for Vin and share GND.
  • SD tied high, GAIN tied low. SD left floating drifts into shutdown and the amp goes quiet at random. GAIN to GND gives 12 dB; the level is set in firmware, not here.
  • Neither LRA lead goes to ground. The coil sits across the amp's + and − output terminals only. Grounding one side shorts half the bridge.

The amp's header reads LRC · BCLK · DIN · GAIN · SD · GND · Vin down the edge, in that order. If you've soldered the header on the other side, the order is the same read from the same end.

Do it

  1. Unplug USB. The amp must never see power before SD and GAIN are strapped.
  2. Seat the amp breakout in the breadboard with its header pins facing the XIAO and the green terminal block facing away.
  3. Strap pins first: GAIN → − rail (⑨), SD → + rail (⑧).
  4. Power: GND → − rail (⑦), then Vin → + rail (⑥). Using a separate supply? Its GND goes to the − rail too; only Vin comes from the supply.
  5. Signals: D10 → LRC green (③), D9 → BCLK blue (④), D8 → DIN orange (⑤). These three are the ones that get swapped; check them twice.
  6. Loosen the two terminal screws, push one LRA lead into each, tighten. Tug each lead gently.
  7. Plug USB in. The amp is silent until the dashboard asks for a drive, because the firmware only starts the I²S clock during a run and stops it after. No clock, no drive.
The firmware bounds every drive so you can't leave a tone running. Tone and sweeps stop themselves after 3 s or at the end of the step table, and there's a 1.5 s cooldown before the next long run. A continuous sine at resonance puts about 0.5 W into an 8 Ω coil rated for far less on average; music-like signals average around 80 mW. If the LRA gets warm to the touch, stop. If it feels harder rather than louder when you step Vol up, that's clipping: step back down.

What you'll see

1Press Tap: the dashboard's peak jumps above 0 and the amp readout says DRIVING. That's the "amp takes the signal" test. Peak stays 0 = the board never produced samples: firmware or I²S pins.
2You feel the tap. Hold the LRA between two fingertips. Peak > 0 but nothing felt = amp power, SD strap, or the coil isn't in the terminals.
3Tone 160 Hz is distinctly stronger than the tap. Three seconds, then it stops on its own. Walk Vol up until it stops getting stronger; back off one step.
4The wide sweep gets strong somewhere between 130 and 190 Hz and fades above 250. Press Mark this step at the strongest point. If the strongest point is way off, the actuator is loaded by whatever it's touching, or it's not the HF variant.
5The FSR and pulse traces don't get fuzzier while the amp runs. Automatic test. If they do, the amp is pulling the 3.3 V rail around: separate supply for Vin, or a 100 µF cap across Vin and GND at the amp.
6Tap on every heartbeat. Finger on the pulse sensor, switch it on. A tap per beat, 80 ms each. This is the first Amulet behaviour running end to end: sensed heartbeat in, felt beat out.

Test it the moment it's wired

The Haptics panel is the bottom section of the dashboard at http://localhost:8787: Tap, Tone, two sweeps, Stop, Vol, and the heartbeat toggle. It needs the bench3_live firmware; if the panel says NO STATUS, the board is still on bench2 and needs Bench 3 Dashboard.command.
