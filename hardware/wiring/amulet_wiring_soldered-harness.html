Amulet Soldered Harness

Everything from the bench, soldered wire-to-pad with no custom PCB. The only board you add is a scrap of protoboard or stripboard two rows wide, because the XIAO has one 3V3 pin and one GND pin and several things need each. The amp's power is the exception: it comes straight off the switched battery lead, because its coil current pulls the XIAO's 3.3 V regulator down and corrupts every sensor (measured 14 Sep). The FSR's divider resistor lives on the sensor lead under heat-shrink, so there are no junctions anywhere else. Pins and firmware are unchanged from the bench: flash bench3_live and the dashboard works over USB while you build.

Three decisions that make it small

  • One power bus, not a star. A stripboard scrap with a 3V3 row and a GND row. One wire from the XIAO to each row; every sensor power lead lands on the bus. Two joints on the XIAO instead of nine.
  • The amp is powered from the battery, not the bus. Its Vin goes to the switched battery +. GND stays on the bus so everything shares a ground. On the bench with USB, use the XIAO's 5V pin for the same reason. Set SUPPLY_VOLTS in the firmware to 4.2 on battery or 5.0 on USB so the volume ceiling still protects the coil.
  • Resistor on the lead, not on a board. The FSR's 10 kΩ is soldered inline right at the sensor tail, then heat-shrunk. The junction is the solder blob.
  • The amp's spare pads are used as jumpers. SD to Vin and GAIN to GND are two short wire loops on the back of the amp board. GAIN can go straight to the bus GND row instead, which the diagram shows.

Build order

Each stage is testable on the dashboard before the next one goes on. Keep the USB-C plugged in for testing; the battery is last.

  1. Make the bus. Cut a stripboard scrap two copper rows wide and about ten holes long, plus room for the 6 mm button at one end. Check with a meter that the two rows are not shorted. Tin both rows. Solder the button in with one leg pair on the GND row and the other pair on an isolated pad; the teal D2 wire goes to that pad.
  2. XIAO power. Solder red from the 3V3 castellation to the top row, black from GND to the bottom row. Plug in USB, confirm 3.3 V between the rows.
  3. Amp. Solder the SD to Vin loop on the back first, while the board is bare. Then GAIN and GND to the bus GND row, then the three I²S wires from D10, D9, D8. Vin gets a red lead left long; for bench testing land it on the XIAO 5V pin, and at step 8 move it to the switch output. Screw or solder the LRA to the output. Press LRA ON in the dashboard. If it buzzes and the FSR number does not dip while it runs, the amp is done.
  4. Trunks. Two long leads from the bus rows, red and black, running to where the sensors will be. Leave them long; trim after the layout is settled.
  5. Force strip. Pin A to red trunk. At pin B, solder the yellow signal lead and one leg of the 10 kΩ into the same joint. Heat-shrink the resistor body. Other leg to a black lead, to the GND trunk. Yellow to D0. Dashboard: rest reading and a press.
  6. Pulse sensor. Three pads: + red, − black, S purple to D1. Finger on, watch for BEATING.
  7. Battery. Unplug USB. Slide switch in the + lead. From the switch output, two wires: one to the BAT+ pad on the underside of the XIAO, one to amp Vin. − to BAT−. Switch on; the board should boot and the dashboard reconnect when USB is plugged in again.

Soldering to the boards

XIAO castellations

Tin the castellation first, then lay the pre-tinned wire into the half-hole from the outside and reflow for two seconds. Do not push the wire into the through-hole; it will bridge to the underside pad next to it. Eight joints, all on the edges.

BAT pads are on the underside, near the USB end, marked + and −. Small, close together. Tin, touch, done. Check for a short between them before connecting the cell.

Wire

AWG30 silicone for everything. You have 15 m of yellow. Signal runs carry microamps and the LRA burst is 150 mA for a second, which AWG30 handles over these lengths. Mark the ends with tape or a pen if you only have one colour, and follow the colour key in the table.

Strip 2 mm, tin, cut to 1 mm. Short tinned ends do not bridge neighbouring pads.

Inline resistor

Trim the 10 kΩ legs to 4 mm. One leg goes into the FSR pin-B joint together with the signal wire. Slide heat-shrink over the body before soldering the second leg to its black lead. This turns a three-way junction into one solid, insulated lump at the sensor tail.

Strain relief

Every wire leaving a board gets a dab of hot glue or a strip of Kapton over the joint. Castellation joints in particular will lift a pad if the wire is tugged. On the glove, the wire should be anchored to fabric within 2 cm of each joint.

Before the battery goes on. With USB unplugged and the switch off, meter between the bus rows: it must read open, not a short. Then between BAT+ and BAT− on the XIAO: open. Then between the amp Vin joint and the bus GND row: open. A dead short across a LiPo is the one mistake that damages parts. Why the amp is on the battery: with amp Vin on the 3V3 pin, a continuous LRA drive dropped the rail about 0.3 V with 160 Hz ripple; the FSR fell 10 %, the pulse channel swung rail to rail and produced phantom beats, and the skin channel's noise tripled. Off the battery lead, the regulator never sees the coil current.
Layout that keeps the wires short. XIAO and bus side by side on the back of the hand, amp next to them, LRA on the inside of the wrist. Battery under the XIAO. Force strip along the index finger, pulse sensor on the index fingertip of the other hand's glove or the ring finger of this one. The two trunks run down the back of the hand to a single junction point near the base of the fingers, and the short sensor leads fan out from there.
